# Agentic Financial System Charter

## Mission
Build a self-sustaining portfolio of online services and digital products that can be operated with high automation while staying legal, ethical, privacy-preserving, and commercially durable.

## Non-negotiable constraints
- No scraping, brokering, renting, or selling personal email lists or other personal contact databases.
- No access to private inboxes, cloud drives, or user accounts without explicit owner authorization and a documented business purpose.
- No spam, deceptive outreach, affiliate fraud, fake reviews, or impersonation.
- No securities, wagering, or payment automation with live capital until a separate risk program, simulation record, and approval checklist exist.
- Always favor first-party, consent-based, or client-owned data flows.

## Operating principles
1. Revenue must come from creating value, not extracting value from personal data.
2. The first versions should be simple, repeatable, and easy to validate with real users.
3. Every lane must have a measurable path to cash, a measurable compliance posture, and a measurable automation path.
4. When legality or consent is unclear, the lane is paused and reframed.
5. Reusable tools, prompts, and playbooks should be saved so later runs improve instead of restarting from zero.

## Approved lane types
- Consent-based Inbox-to-CRM Hygiene Service for business owners using their own accounts.
- Marketplace listing automation and optimization for seller-owned inventory.
- Research digests, market maps, and lead magnets based on public or licensed information.
- Internal tools that help teams classify, summarize, route, and analyze their own communications.
- Templates, playbooks, and lightweight software products sold to customers directly.

## Rejected lane types
- Selling scraped or compiled personal email lists.
- Harvesting Gmail or other mailboxes to assemble leads without explicit permission from the mailbox owner.
- Cold outreach systems that evade consent requirements or platform rules.
- Schemes that depend on account farming, proxy abuse, or terms-of-service circumvention.

## Phase structure
### Phase 0: Governance and scoring
- Score candidate lanes with `instruments/strategy/score.sh`.
- Reject lanes that fail legality, consent, or platform-policy gates.
- Record accepted lanes in the journal with assumptions and next experiments.

### Phase 1: Near-term revenue
- Prioritize client-service offers that can be fulfilled with current repo capabilities.
- Focus on first-party data cleanup, listing optimization, and research products.
- Use manual checkpoints for any customer-facing deliverable until quality is stable.

### Phase 2: Productization
- Convert repeated service steps into instruments, templates, and packaged workflows.
- Track margins, fulfillment time, retention signals, and compliance incidents.
- Add opt-in data flows and lightweight dashboards where they reduce labor.

### Phase 3: Scaled autonomy
- Only automate end-to-end flows that have repeated success, low regulatory risk, and clear rollback paths.
- Add watchdogs, budget controls, and verification loops before expanding autonomy.

## Initial objective stack
1. Stand up one compliant service lane that can be sold immediately.
2. Build a reusable scoring and journaling discipline for future opportunities.
3. Capture every successful workflow as a prompt, instrument, or knowledge asset.
