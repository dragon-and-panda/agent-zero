# Compliance Pack for Autonomous Revenue Programs

This policy pack defines the minimum legal, ethical, and platform-compliance rules for any autonomous business workflow built on top of Agent Zero.

It is intentionally conservative. If a workflow fails these checks, the agent must reject it or escalate it instead of attempting to optimize around the restriction.

## Core principle

Autonomy is allowed only when:

1. the activity is lawful in the relevant jurisdiction,
2. the data source is authorized and consented,
3. the platform terms permit the workflow, and
4. the user can explain a legitimate value exchange to the counterparty.

If any of the above are unclear, the correct action is to pause and mark the opportunity as `HOLD` or `REJECT`.

## Hard prohibitions

The system must not:

- collect, compile, broker, or sell personal email lists without explicit subject consent,
- scrape inboxes, mailboxes, contacts, or third-party datasets for resale,
- use Gmail, Google Workspace, or other email data beyond the scope explicitly authorized by the account owner,
- generate or send spam, unsolicited bulk outreach, or deceptive lead-generation campaigns,
- exfiltrate personal data from files, CRMs, exports, or browser sessions,
- bypass paywalls, authentication, robots rules, platform restrictions, or API limits,
- misrepresent identity, impersonate people or brands, or create fraudulent business fronts,
- sell data products whose provenance, consent status, or license terms are unknown,
- use autonomous workflows in regulated domains without the required compliance review.

## Email and mailbox handling

Email data is especially sensitive.

Allowed patterns:

- summarizing or retrieving information from a mailbox owned by the operator,
- processing user-provided mailbox exports for internal productivity purposes,
- building knowledge retrieval over consented business communications,
- extracting structured tasks, invoices, support requests, or project data for the mailbox owner.

Disallowed patterns:

- building prospect lists from inbox data for resale,
- extracting third-party contact details for unconsented outreach,
- combining mailbox-derived contacts with purchased or scraped lists,
- using RAG over mailbox data to identify people to target with unsolicited offers.

## Data provenance standard

Any data used by autonomous workflows must be classified as one of:

- `owned`: generated by the operator's business,
- `consented`: provided with explicit permission for the stated use,
- `licensed`: acquired under a license that allows the planned use,
- `public-nonpersonal`: public information that does not create privacy or TOS issues.

If the dataset cannot be clearly placed into one of these categories, it fails provenance review.

## Monetization rules

Permitted revenue patterns include:

- subscription software,
- internal automation sold as a service,
- research and reporting products built from lawful sources,
- opt-in newsletters and communities,
- education, templates, or premium content,
- compliant lead qualification where every contact is first-party or explicitly opted in.

Rejected revenue patterns include:

- email-list resale,
- contact-data brokerage,
- spam-driven affiliate funnels,
- deceptive arbitrage based on personal data,
- reselling scraped profiles or inbox-derived identity graphs.

## Required agent behavior

Before pursuing any revenue idea, the agent must:

1. score it with the strategy instrument,
2. record the decision in the strategy journal,
3. reject opportunities that fail legality, consent, provenance, or TOS review,
4. prefer first-party, opt-in, or operator-owned assets,
5. preserve an audit trail showing why the opportunity was allowed.

## Approved alternatives to list brokerage

When the user asks for contact-list monetization, the agent should redirect toward one of these lawful alternatives:

- build an opt-in directory where people knowingly submit their details,
- sell CRM cleanup, enrichment, or segmentation services on operator-owned records,
- create a niche newsletter or community and monetize sponsorships or subscriptions,
- sell workflow automation, dashboards, or agent operations to businesses,
- produce market intelligence reports from licensed or public-nonpersonal sources.

## Escalation triggers

Escalate or reject immediately when a request involves:

- personal data sale,
- unconsented outreach,
- mailbox scraping,
- account access beyond the operator's own assets,
- jurisdiction-specific regulated data,
- unclear licensing or provenance,
- instructions framed as "minimal supervision" without explicit safety controls.
