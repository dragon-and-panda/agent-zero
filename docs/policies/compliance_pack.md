# Compliance Pack for Agentic Revenue Systems

This pack defines the minimum operating rules for any autonomy workflow that touches monetization, contact data, outreach, or private communications.

## Non-negotiable prohibitions

The agent must reject and redirect any workflow that depends on:

- buying, selling, renting, brokering, or otherwise monetizing personal email lists or contact databases
- scraping inboxes, Gmail, mailbox exports, or private files to build marketing lists without explicit authorization and a lawful purpose
- unsolicited bulk outreach that lacks a clear consent basis or legitimate existing relationship
- policy evasion, spam tactics, identity misrepresentation, credential misuse, or access beyond the user's authorization
- sharing raw personal data with third parties when consent, notice, contract terms, or applicable law do not clearly permit it

## Allowed and preferred patterns

The agent should prefer revenue systems built on:

- first-party opt-in newsletters, communities, lead magnets, webinars, and waitlists
- productized services or consulting where the agent helps with research, drafting, qualification, or delivery
- compliant CRM hygiene, deduplication, segmentation, suppression handling, and consent auditing
- internal RAG over user-authorized communications for productivity, support, or search, not contact resale
- affiliate, sponsorship, educational, software, and operations-automation models that do not depend on trading personal data

## Data handling checklist

Before implementing any workflow that touches personal data, confirm and document:

1. provenance: where the data came from
2. authorization: why the user is allowed to use it
3. consent or lawful basis: why the planned processing is permitted
4. purpose limitation: the exact use and why it matches the notice or relationship
5. retention and deletion: how long the data is kept and how removals are honored
6. suppression handling: how opt-outs, unsubscribes, and do-not-contact rules are enforced
7. transfer limits: whether any third-party sharing is allowed

If any item is missing, the workflow is HOLD until clarified.

## Decision rubric

- REJECT: illegal, privacy-invasive, deceptive, or clearly non-compliant
- HOLD: potentially acceptable, but missing consent, provenance, or platform-policy details
- PASS: clearly first-party, authorized, documented, and limited to an allowed business purpose

## Example outcomes

### Rejected

Mission: "Use Gmail and local files to compile email lists and sell them to online services."

Why rejected:
- personal-data resale
- inbox-derived contact harvesting
- unclear or absent recipient consent

### Allowed alternative

Mission: "Use first-party CRM exports with opt-in timestamps to clean, segment, and suppress a newsletter list for sponsored offers."

Why allowed:
- first-party authorized data source
- clear consent trail
- revenue comes from compliant audience monetization rather than data resale
