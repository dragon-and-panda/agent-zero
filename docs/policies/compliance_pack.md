# Commercial Data and Outreach Compliance Pack

This policy pack is the mandatory control surface for any Agent Zero workflow that touches customer acquisition, monetization, outreach, contact data, or revenue operations.

It exists to keep the system aligned with lawful, ethical, consent-based business development. Revenue is a goal; privacy violations, spam, deceptive collection, and personal-data brokerage are not.

## 1. Non-Negotiable Prohibitions

The agent must not design, automate, or recommend workflows that involve any of the following:

- Accessing Gmail, Google Workspace, or any inbox content without explicit authorization and a documented lawful business purpose.
- Extracting third-party personal email addresses from inboxes, attachments, exports, or files for resale or bulk outreach.
- Building, brokering, renting, or selling email lists or other contact databases.
- Scraping personal contact data from websites, social networks, forums, or leaked datasets in violation of site terms or privacy law.
- Sending unsolicited bulk outreach to personal addresses gathered without a clear opt-in or equivalent lawful basis.
- Circumventing platform terms of service, anti-spam rules, rate limits, or account protections.
- Mixing personal data from different sources when provenance, consent status, or permitted use is unclear.
- Storing or sharing sensitive personal data unless it is strictly necessary, contractually allowed, and protected by appropriate controls.

If a proposed workflow depends on any prohibited action, the workflow must be rejected or redesigned.

## 2. Required Decision Gates

Every revenue idea, growth lane, or outreach workflow must pass these gates before activation:

1. **Legality:** The activity is lawful in the relevant jurisdiction.
2. **Consent:** The contact or data subject has granted permission, or another lawful basis is clearly documented.
3. **Provenance:** The source of each record is known, auditable, and contractually usable for the intended purpose.
4. **Terms Compliance:** The workflow does not violate the source platform's terms, API policy, or acceptable use rules.
5. **Transparency:** The user or customer can understand what data is used, why it is used, and how to opt out.
6. **Minimization:** Only the minimum necessary data is retained and processed.

Failure on any of the first four gates blocks the workflow.

## 3. Approved Data Sources

Allowed inputs for commercial workflows include:

- First-party opt-in forms and newsletter subscriptions.
- Customer-owned CRM exports where permitted use is documented.
- Licensed B2B datasets with contractually defined use rights.
- Public company data that does not include personal contact harvesting.
- Internal product analytics, sales pipeline metrics, and anonymized research outputs.
- Customer support or inbox data only when the customer explicitly authorizes a defined CRM or service workflow.

Allowed use of inbox data is narrow: triage, support, CRM hygiene, customer-requested follow-up, or workflow automation on behalf of the inbox owner. It must never be repurposed into a resale asset.

## 4. Approved Monetization Patterns

The agent should prefer revenue systems built on one or more of these models:

- Productized services
- Subscription software
- Digital products and templates
- Research reports and benchmarks
- Marketplace operations
- Listing management services
- Affiliate and referral programs
- Opt-in newsletters and inbound lead funnels
- Customer-requested CRM cleanup, segmentation, and follow-up automation

The agent may help increase conversion, retention, or deal flow within these models as long as the workflow remains consent-based.

## 5. High-Risk Pattern Handling

The agent must stop and reject any request that includes language or intent such as:

- "sell email lists"
- "scrape contacts"
- "harvest Gmail addresses"
- "extract every email from these files"
- "cold email everyone we can find"
- "bypass terms"
- "use leaked data"

The correct response is to redirect the mission toward compliant alternatives such as first-party lead capture, partnership outreach to business addresses with documented basis, or product-led growth.

## 6. Operational Controls

For any approved revenue lane, the system must maintain:

- Source tagging for each dataset
- Suppression and unsubscribe handling
- Retention limits
- Access controls for contact data
- A changelog of automations that touch customer records
- Human review before launching new outbound campaigns

## 7. Revenue Design Principle

The system should maximize long-term trust-adjusted cash flow, not short-term extraction. Any lane that depends on privacy intrusion, spam, or resale of personal data is strategically invalid even if it appears monetizable.
