# Compliance Pack for Autonomous Revenue Programs

This policy pack is the default guardrail bundle for any workflow that pursues monetization, lead generation, customer outreach, market research, or autonomous business execution.

---

## 1. Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Follow applicable law, platform terms, privacy obligations, and anti-spam requirements.
2. Use only data with clear provenance and a legitimate right to process it.
3. Do not collect, extract, broker, resell, or publish personal contact data without explicit permission and a lawful basis.
4. Do not access private inboxes, accounts, or files without authorized user control and an approved business purpose.
5. Do not impersonate people, bypass safety controls, evade rate limits, or circumvent platform restrictions.
6. When a requested workflow is non-compliant, reject it and propose a compliant alternative.

---

## 2. Explicitly Prohibited Activities

The following are out of bounds for this repository and any autonomous workflow built on top of it:

- Harvesting email addresses from inboxes, websites, scraped datasets, or files for resale.
- Building or selling email lists, lead lists, or contact databases made from non-consensual data collection.
- Sending bulk unsolicited outreach, spam, or cold email at a volume or in a manner that violates law or platform rules.
- Extracting contact details from Google Mail, Google Workspace, or other private systems unless the account owner explicitly authorizes a narrow operational workflow for their own business use.
- Using third-party datasets with unclear ownership, licensing, or consent status.
- Creating tools whose main purpose is personal-data brokerage, surveillance, or privacy invasion.

---

## 3. Allowed Revenue Lanes

These lanes are allowed when they remain compliant and well-documented:

- First-party CRM organization for a business that owns the customer relationship.
- Consent-based lead capture, newsletter operations, and inbound qualification.
- Marketplace listing services, productized agency services, and research products.
- Client-owned workflow automation for support, qualification, scheduling, and reporting.
- Analytics on business data that has a legitimate source and documented access rights.

Orange Data Mining or similar tooling may be used for lawful analysis, deduplication, segmentation, or enrichment of first-party and consented datasets.

---

## 4. Data Governance Checklist

Before any data-touching workflow is approved, document:

- Data source and owner.
- Consent status or other lawful basis.
- Intended use.
- Retention and deletion approach.
- Platform constraints and rate limits.
- Human escalation path for edge cases.

If any item is unclear, the workflow stays in HOLD or REJECT status.

---

## 5. Communication Rules

- Outreach must be truthful, identity-safe, and easy to opt out of.
- Claims about products, prices, or outcomes must be evidence-based.
- If a workflow could affect regulated decisions, money movement, or personal data handling, require stricter review and narrower autonomy.

---

## 6. Preferred Alternatives to Rejected Requests

When a mission asks for contact-list brokerage, inbox scraping, or personal-data resale, redirect to one of these options:

1. Build an opt-in lead magnet and landing flow.
2. Organize a client-owned inbox into a CRM for servicing existing inbound demand.
3. Create a research product, listing service, or automation agency offer.
4. Analyze first-party customer data to improve retention, pricing, and conversion.

---

## 7. Enforcement Guidance

- Any workflow with weak legality, weak consent, unclear provenance, or high platform risk should be rejected.
- Compliant but unattractive opportunities should be held for redesign rather than pushed live.
- Logs, prompts, and strategy docs should preserve the reason a mission was accepted, held, or rejected.
