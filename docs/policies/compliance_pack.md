# Compliance Pack for Autonomous Revenue Systems

This policy pack defines the minimum rules for any Agent Zero workflow that is intended to generate revenue, acquire customers, process data, or automate outreach.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Operate lawfully and ethically in every jurisdiction and platform involved.
- Require clear authorization before accessing any account, mailbox, file set, or dataset.
- Use the minimum data necessary for the task.
- Prefer first-party, opt-in, or properly licensed data over scraped or third-party personal data.
- Create value through products, services, research, or automation; do not monetize access to personal information.

## Prohibited activities

- Scraping or mining inboxes, drives, CRM exports, or files to build personal contact lists for sale.
- Selling, brokering, renting, swapping, or packaging personal email addresses or other personal data.
- Sending spam or automating outreach without a lawful basis, consent where required, and platform compliance.
- Circumventing website terms, access controls, rate limits, or anti-bot protections.
- Using stolen credentials, intercepted content, deceptive identity claims, or impersonation.
- Building workflows whose main purpose is surveillance, doxxing, credential harvesting, fraud, or privacy invasion.

## Allowed patterns

- Owner-authorized inbox automation for classification, summarization, support triage, reminders, or analytics.
- Retrieval over private data when the account owner authorized access and the output stays within the owner's environment.
- Analysis of anonymized, aggregated, synthetic, public-domain, or properly licensed datasets.
- Consent-based lead generation using first-party forms, waitlists, newsletters, and customer referrals.
- Monetization via software, subscriptions, consulting, agencies, content, education, marketplaces, or affiliate workflows that do not depend on personal-data resale.

## Required checks before launch

1. Authorization: do we have the right to access the account or dataset?
2. Consent: are recipients or data subjects opted in where needed?
3. Provenance: is the data first-party, public, licensed, or otherwise lawful to use?
4. Platform rules: do the acquisition and automation methods comply with site terms and API rules?
5. Retention: are we storing only what is necessary, for a defined purpose?
6. User value: are we selling a real product/service instead of exploiting contact data?

## Safe alternatives to rejected ideas

If a proposed workflow depends on scraping personal email addresses or selling contact data, replace it with one of these:

- Build a consent-based newsletter or waitlist.
- Sell a productized service that solves a business problem for opted-in customers.
- Use owner-authorized inbox RAG for support, operations, or executive summaries.
- Create content, software, templates, or affiliate assets that attract first-party demand.
- Use public company data and lawful market research to identify prospects, then contact them only through compliant channels.

## Default decision policy

- Reject plans that require personal-data resale, spam, or unauthorized account access.
- Hold plans that need legal review, clearer consent, or better provenance documentation.
- Proceed only when legality, authorization, consent, and platform compliance are all reasonably satisfied.
