# Compliance Pack: Consent-First Revenue Operations

This policy pack defines the minimum standards for any autonomous revenue or growth workflow running inside Agent Zero.

## 1. Operating principle

Revenue systems must rely on legal, ethical, and platform-compliant acquisition. If a strategy depends on deception, unauthorized data access, privacy invasion, or resale of personal data, it is out of scope.

## 2. Explicitly prohibited

- Selling, renting, brokering, or swapping personal email lists or contact databases.
- Extracting contacts from Gmail, inboxes, CRMs, spreadsheets, or files for resale, cold outreach, or enrichment without an explicit lawful basis and user authorization.
- Scraping personal data from websites, communities, or accounts in ways that violate terms of service, privacy law, or reasonable user expectations.
- Sending spam, evading platform safeguards, bypassing rate limits, or using deceptive growth tactics.
- Storing or expanding dossiers of personal data beyond what is necessary for the active task.
- Using regulated or sensitive data involving finance, health, minors, or government records without a separate compliance review.

## 3. Allowed revenue lanes

- First-party, opt-in newsletters and lead magnets with clear disclosure and unsubscribe flows.
- Digital products such as templates, research packs, datasets built from owned or licensed sources, and automation playbooks.
- Service businesses such as listing optimization, workflow automation, analytics, and AI implementation.
- Marketplace tooling that helps a seller improve copy, pricing, routing, and operations for assets they already own.
- User-authorized inbox or CRM organization performed for the account owner's internal use only.

## 4. Required checks before launch

Every monetization plan should be evaluated for:

1. Legality
2. Consent quality
3. Data provenance
4. Platform and marketplace rules
5. Data minimization
6. Auditability
7. Economic repeatability

If any of the first four checks fail, the workflow should be rejected or paused.

## 5. Handling email and inbox data

- Gmail or mailbox data may only be used for owner-authorized internal operations such as summarization, categorization, follow-up drafting, or first-party CRM hygiene.
- Contact data derived from inboxes may not be packaged as a product for resale.
- Any outreach must be permission-based and tied to a legitimate business relationship or opt-in record.

## 6. Safer alternatives when a plan is rejected

When a strategy is rejected for privacy or consent reasons, pivot to one of these models:

- Build an opt-in lead capture asset and nurture sequence.
- Sell a service, not a contact list.
- Package knowledge, workflows, or software into a digital product.
- Use Gmail data only to help the owner organize relationships they already have.
- Focus on marketplace or operations automation rather than data brokerage.

## 7. Legal note

This pack is an operational safeguard, not legal advice. When a strategy touches regulated data, cross-border messaging, or sector-specific rules, escalate for legal review before execution.
