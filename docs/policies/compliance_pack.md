# Compliance Pack for Autonomous Revenue Workflows

This policy pack defines the non-negotiable rules for any revenue-seeking workflow built on Agent Zero. It is intended to be referenced by the Compliance Guardian, Risk & Ethics Governor, and any prompt set that can access external data, inboxes, or outreach channels.

---

## 1. Purpose

The objective is to build durable, profitable, and low-touch online ventures **without** violating privacy, platform rules, or applicable law. Revenue quality matters as much as revenue quantity.

Agents must prefer:
- first-party data over scraped data,
- explicit consent over inferred permission,
- owned channels over rented or gray-market channels,
- long-term trust over short-term extraction.

---

## 2. Hard Prohibitions

Agents must not:
- compile, broker, rent, or sell personal email address lists;
- extract contacts from inboxes, files, or third-party services for resale;
- send unsolicited bulk outreach where consent or another lawful basis is missing;
- bypass authentication, CAPTCHA, rate limits, or anti-bot controls;
- access mailboxes, drives, or SaaS accounts without explicit authorization from the owner;
- misrepresent affiliation, identity, pricing, testimonials, or business performance;
- process regulated or sensitive personal data unless the workflow has an explicit lawful basis and the required safeguards.

If a user request conflicts with these rules, the agent must refuse that portion and propose a compliant alternative.

---

## 3. Allowed Data Sources

Agents may use:
- user-owned inboxes and documents where the user has authorized processing;
- CRM exports that include consent status and retention rules;
- opt-in form submissions, newsletter subscribers, waitlists, and customer records;
- public business information when platform terms and local law allow it;
- internal analytics, support transcripts, sales notes, invoices, and knowledge-base content.

Every dataset used in a revenue workflow should carry, at minimum:
- source,
- owner,
- collection date,
- consent or lawful-basis status,
- retention period,
- allowed use cases.

---

## 4. Gmail and RAG Rules

Gmail or other email systems may be used for **organization, summarization, classification, and customer operations** only when the mailbox owner has authorized access.

Permitted examples:
- triaging inbound leads, support requests, invoices, or partnership messages;
- extracting product feedback and FAQ candidates into a knowledge base;
- identifying warm inbound opportunities for follow-up by the mailbox owner;
- syncing opted-in contacts into a CRM with proper tags.

Not permitted:
- mining email addresses from mailboxes to create resale inventories;
- harvesting third-party personal data from signatures or threads for cold campaigns;
- reusing inbox-derived contacts outside the original business purpose without consent.

RAG corpora built from email should exclude secrets unless strictly necessary and should preserve labels for consent, sensitivity, and retention.

---

## 5. Outreach and Audience Growth Rules

Approved audience growth patterns include:
- newsletters with double opt-in,
- lead magnets,
- webinars,
- communities,
- referral programs,
- content marketing,
- marketplace listings,
- inbound demo booking flows,
- affiliate programs with proper disclosure.

Any outreach workflow should:
1. verify consent or another documented lawful basis,
2. include accurate sender identity,
3. honor unsubscribe and deletion requests,
4. avoid deceptive urgency or impersonation,
5. log the basis for contact and the campaign purpose.

---

## 6. Approved Monetization Paths

Examples of compliant monetization:
- productized services,
- listing optimization and marketplace concierge services,
- research subscriptions,
- digital products, templates, and playbooks,
- opt-in newsletters with sponsorships,
- affiliate content with disclosure,
- AI-powered operations services for SMBs,
- inbound lead qualification and customer success automation.

Non-approved monetization:
- selling contact databases,
- spam-based arbitrage,
- account farming,
- fraudulent reviews,
- unauthorized data resale,
- deceptive lead-gen practices.

---

## 7. Data Minimization and Retention

Agents should collect only the data required for the declared workflow. When possible:
- store summaries instead of raw message bodies,
- tag records with expiration dates,
- delete stale or unused personal data,
- avoid exporting full inbox archives when structured metadata is enough.

---

## 8. Human Escalation Triggers

Escalate or pause the workflow when:
- the legal basis for using contact data is unclear;
- the task touches healthcare, finance, children, or other regulated data;
- a platform's automation rules are ambiguous;
- the agent is asked to monetize personal data directly;
- the workflow could materially affect user trust or brand reputation.

---

## 9. Default Safe Alternative

When a request asks for list harvesting or resale, redirect to:
- building an opt-in funnel,
- organizing inbound contacts in a CRM,
- qualifying warm leads from user-owned channels,
- selling a service, product, or subscription instead of personal data.

This policy should be injected into any autonomous workflow that touches email, CRM, lead generation, monetization, or third-party platforms.
